Posture monitoring, no hardware required

Your webcam already sees how you sit.

ThinkPulse watches your back and neck through your laptop camera: the forward head lean, the downward bend from looking at a phone or a low screen, the rounded shoulders. It ignores your legs entirely. Everything runs in your browser, and no video ever leaves your device.

Try the live demo

How it works

Three steps, all inside your browser tab.

01

Camera reads your position

A pose model finds your shoulders, ears, and nose in each video frame, on your device.

02

Angle gets calculated

The same two measurements physiotherapists use for forward head posture and rounded shoulders, adapted for a front-facing camera, are compared against your own upright baseline. Legs are not part of the score.

03

You get told, not scolded

A quiet status line changes when your posture slips, and a sitting timer tracks how long you've been at it.

Explore

Lean left or right to look around.

A few things worth knowing about how ThinkPulse works. Use the arrows or dots below, or swipe an open palm while the camera is on. See the gesture guide in the live demo above.

Built for long sessions

Study blocks, work sprints, long calls, ThinkPulse is meant to run quietly in a tab for hours, not for a one-off check.

No hardware, no account

No belt, no sensor kit, no sign-up. Open the page, allow the camera, and the scoring starts on whatever device you're already using.

Calibrated to you

Everyone's neutral posture looks a little different. Setting your own upright baseline is what makes the score mean something for your body, not an average one.

Private by default

Every frame is read and scored in your browser and thrown away immediately after. Nothing about how you sit is ever sent anywhere.

Privacy

Last updated 11 November 2026

ThinkPulse is a prototype made by Arpit Singh. Its camera demo runs entirely in your browser: your camera stream is read by your browser and analyzed on your own device. This page says exactly what that involves, what is stored, and which outside services are contacted and when.

What ThinkPulse receives

  • Nothing. This page has no backend, no database, no account system and no analytics. ThinkPulse never receives your video, your posture scores or anything else about you.
  • Your camera video is turned into body-point positions on your device and discarded frame by frame. Nothing is recorded, screenshotted or uploaded. It is not used to recognize or identify anyone, and no face recognition is involved.

What is stored in your browser

  • Always, in memory only and gone when you close the tab: your sitting-reminder minutes, your upright-posture calibration, and your posture history for the current visit.
  • One cookie, tp_consent, which remembers whether you pressed Accept or Decline. It lasts 12 months and contains only that one word. It is strictly necessary so that you are not asked on every visit.
  • Only if you press Accept, in this browser's local storage: tp_theme (your light or dark choice) and tp_daily (one average posture score per day, for the Spine Timeline). They never leave your browser. If you press Decline, none of this is saved and anything saved earlier is deleted. You can also erase the history with the Clear my history button on that section.

Outside services, and when they are contacted

When you only read this page, no outside service is contacted. The services below are contacted only after a specific action by you. Like any web request, they can see your IP address and browser details, under their own privacy policies.

  • After you press Accept: Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com) delivers the IBM Plex typeface. If you decline, the page uses your device's own fonts and Google is not contacted.
  • When you press Enable camera: jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) delivers the TensorFlow.js, pose-detection and MediaPipe Hands code, and the hand model files. The pose model's trained weights are downloaded from Google's model hosting. These downloads bring files to your browser. None of them receive your camera feed.
  • When you press Generate pairing code: cdnjs (run by Cloudflare) delivers the QR code library and jsDelivr delivers the messaging library. Your browser then connects to a public test relay run by HiveMQ (broker.hivemq.com) to carry a short alert message to your phone under a random session ID. No video and no scores travel through it. It is a public relay and not a private channel: anyone who learned the session ID could read or send those messages, which is why the ID is long and random and is used for one session only. The phone that opens your link connects to the same relay.
  • The Instagram link in the footer leaves this site. Instagram's own policies apply there.

Children

ThinkPulse is built with students in mind. It collects no personal information, has no accounts and shows no ads. If you are under 18, please ask a parent or guardian before you turn the camera on.

Your rights and contact

Because ThinkPulse holds no personal data about you, there is nothing for it to access, correct or delete on its side. Everything it saves is in your own browser, where you can clear it yourself: choose Decline under Cookie settings in the footer, press Clear my history, or clear this site's data in your browser settings. For any question or privacy request, message @calmcircuits.official on Instagram. Gesture guide

50 hand gestures for ThinkPulse.

These work while your camera is running, on this page too. The basics (pointing, pinching, palm swipes, thumbs up, peace sign, fist to pause) are explained in the live demo; this page covers fifty more. Everything is read on your device from your webcam, the same way your posture is.

Before you start

  • Hold still for about half a second. Poses tagged Hold only fire after the hand stays roughly steady, so a pose you pass through while moving does nothing. Swipe and Motion are movements, and Two hands covers both held poses and movements.
  • Left and right are as you see yourself on screen. The camera view is mirrored, so sweeping toward your right hand moves right.
  • Single-hand gestures need one hand in view. With a second hand visible, only the two-hand gestures are read, though the cursor still follows your first hand.
  • Face the camera with good light. Finger detection is approximate. If a pose is not picked up, open the hand a little wider or move closer, and try again.
  • A fist held still pauses everything, and a held open palm resumes it, so you can type or reach for something without triggering anything.
  • The finger diagrams show raised fingers as solid bars and folded fingers as outlines, thumb first, with the hand turned the way the pose is held.
